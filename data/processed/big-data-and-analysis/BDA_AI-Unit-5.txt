Deep Learning on Big Data using TensorFlow on Spark
• Deep Learning requires massive datasets to be accurate.
• Standard TensorFlow runs on a single machine (or single GPU cluster).
• Limitation: Memory constraints (Out of Memory errors) and
impossibly long training times (weeks/months).
The Solution – Best of Both Worlds
• Apache Spark: Handles Data Ingestion, Cleaning, and Feature
Engineering across a cluster.
• TensorFlow: Handles complex tensor mathematics and model
optimization.
• The Goal: Bring the deep learning code directly to the data, rather
than moving the data to the code.
TensorFlowOnSpark (TFoS) Architecture
• Developed and open-sourced by Yahoo.
• Bypasses the need to save Spark data to disk before feeding to
TensorFlow.
• Uses Spark's RDDs/DataFrames to stream data directly into
TensorFlow memory in real-time.
The Distributed Training Workflow
• Step 1: Spark loads raw data (HDFS/S3).
• Step 2: Spark cleans data, handles missing values, and creates vector
formats.
• Step 3: Spark launches a TensorFlow cluster alongside its own
workers.
• Step 4: TF workers compute gradients in parallel and update a central
Parameter Server.
Modern Alternatives (Horovod)
• While TFoS is foundational, Horovod (by Uber) is the modern industry
standard.
• Uses Ring-AllReduce architecture instead of a central parameter
server.
• Requires minimal code changes to scale a standard TF script to
hundreds of GPUs.
Explainable AI (XAI) in Big Data Environments
• The "Black Box" Dilemma
• The Tradeoff: As AI models become more complex and accurate, they
become less interpretable.
• The Risk: Hidden biases, spurious correlations, and catastrophic edge-
case failures.
• The Barrier: Lack of user trust prevents AI adoption in high-stakes
fields.
What is Explainable AI (XAI)?
• Global Explanations: Understanding the overall behavior and rules of
the entire model.
• Local Explanations: Understanding why the model made one specific
decision for one specific data point.
• Goal: Ensure AI is fair, unbiased, and aligned with human values.
Key XAI Techniques: SHAP and LIME
• SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations): Based on game theory;
calculates the exact mathematical contribution of every feature to the
final prediction.
• LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations): Tweaks the
input data slightly to see how the prediction changes, creating a
simple map of the decision.
The Big Data Challenge for XAI
• XAI algorithms are incredibly computationally expensive.
• Calculating exact SHAP values for massive datasets is an NP-hard
problem.
• Running XAI sequentially on a single node is impossible for Big Data.
Scaling XAI with Apache Spark
• Distributed Explainability: Using Spark UDFs (User Defined Functions)
to run SHAP/LIME across a cluster.
• Sampling Strategies: Explaining a statistically significant sample of the
Big Data instead of every single row.
• Libraries: Integrating tools like shap with PySpark DataFrames.
Ethical Issues and Data Governance in Big Data AI
• The Core Ethical Dilemmas in Big Data: Privacy, Bias, and Consent.
• Privacy vs. Utility: The tension between extracting valuable insights
and protecting individual anonymity.
• Algorithmic Bias: Machine Learning amplifies historical human
prejudices.
• Informed Consent: Do users actually understand what they are
agreeing to in 50-page Terms of Service agreements?
Case Studies in Algorithmic Bias
• The Recruiting AI: Trained on 10 years of historical resumes (mostly
male); the AI learned to penalize female candidates.
• Predictive Policing: Historical arrest data leads algorithms to over-
police minority neighborhoods, creating a self-fulfilling feedback loop.
• The Lesson: "Garbage In, Garbage Out" applies to ethics, not just data
quality.
The Myth of Anonymization
• Removing Personally Identifiable Information (PII) like Names and
SSNs is not enough.
• The Netflix Prize Leak: Anonymous movie ratings were cross-
referenced with public IMDb reviews to identify specific users and
their private viewing habits.
• The Solution: Advanced techniques like Differential Privacy and k-
anonymity.
What is Data Governance?
• It is not just IT; it is a business strategy.
• Data Stewardship: Assigning clear
owners who are responsible for specific
datasets.
• Data Lineage: Tracking data from its
origin, through all transformations, to its
final AI model.
• Master Data Management (MDM):
Ensuring a single, accurate version of the
truth across the enterprise.
Regulatory Frameworks (GDPR, CCPA, DPDP)
• GDPR (Europe): The gold standard. Introduces massive fines, the
"Right to be Forgotten," and strict consent rules.
• CCPA (California): Focuses heavily on the right to opt-out of data
sales.
• DPDP Act (India): India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act,
focusing on data minimization and purpose limitation.
Technical Implementation in Big Data
• Apache Ranger: Centralized security framework to manage access
control (who can read/write what) across the Hadoop ecosystem.
• Apache Atlas: Data governance and metadata framework to track
data lineage and catalog assets.
• Audit Logs: Immutable records of exactly which user or AI algorithm
touched a piece of sensitive data.
Edge Computing and AI for Low Latency Applications
• The "Round-Trip" Latency Problem
• Network Latency: The physical time it takes data to travel.
• Bandwidth Choke: Transmitting Terabytes of raw 4K video/sensor
data is impossible over mobile networks.
• Connectivity: What happens if the network goes down?
What is Edge AI?
• Training vs. Inference: We do not train
models on the Edge.
• The Workflow: Train massive models on
Cloud Big Data ->Freeze the model ->
Deploy to the Edge.
• Goal: Sub-millisecond decision making
entirely on local hardware.
How do we fit Big Data AI on tiny devices?
• Edge devices (cameras, sensors) have low RAM, weak CPUs, and run
on batteries.
• Model Quantization: Reducing the precision of the math (e.g.,
changing 32-bit decimals to 8-bit integers) to save memory.
• Model Pruning: Deleting artificial neurons that don't contribute much
to the final answer.
Specialized Edge Hardware
• Standard CPUs are not designed for AI matrix math.
• NPUs (Neural Processing Units): Chips specifically designed to run AI
inference at ultra-low power.
• The "System on a Chip" (SoC): Bringing the camera, memory, and AI
brain onto a single board.
The Edge-to-Cloud Big Data Pipeline
• The Edge does not replace the Cloud; it acts as a smart filter.
• Edge AI processes 99% of "normal" data locally and deletes it.
• It only sends anomalies, metadata, or extremely valuable data back to
the Big Data Cloud for long-term storage and retraining.
Case Study 1: AI-Powered Big Data in Healthcare
• The Healthcare Data Ecosystem (The 4 V's in Action)
• Volume: Petabytes of historical patient records.
• Velocity: Real-time streaming vitals from ICU monitors and wearables.
• Variety: Unstructured text (clinical notes), 3D images (MRI/CT), and
time-series data.
• Veracity: The critical need for accurate, clean, and life-saving data.
Deep Dive 1 – Predictive Analytics in the ICU
• The Problem: Conditions like Sepsis kill rapidly if not caught early.
• The Big Data Solution: Streaming real-time ICU vitals through an
Apache Kafka/Spark pipeline.
• The AI Model: Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) analyzing time-
series data to spot invisible physiological trends.
Deep Dive 2 – Medical Imaging & Diagnostics
• The Bottleneck: Radiologists suffer from high burnout and visual
fatigue.
• The Technology: Distributed Deep Learning (CNNs/Vision
Transformers) trained on millions of historical scans.
• The Workflow: AI acts as a "Tireless Triage," instantly flagging the
highest-risk scans for human review.
Genomics and Precision Medicine
• The Scale: Sequencing a single human genome generates ~100GB of
raw data.
• Population Mining: Finding hidden correlations between specific
genetic mutations and disease risks across millions of patients.
• The Outcome: Moving from "One Size Fits All" treatments to
personalized, genetically targeted therapies.
Roadblocks: Data Silos and Interoperability
• Data Silos: Hospitals use different, proprietary software systems that
cannot talk to each other.
• Interoperability: The struggle to standardize medical data formats
(e.g., FHIR standards).
• The Impact: Fragmented patient histories lead to less accurate AI
models.
Governance and Ethics in Medicine
• Privacy: Strict adherence to HIPAA/GDPR; medical data is the most
sensitive data on earth.
• Bias: Ensuring AI models are trained on diverse populations, not just
wealthy urban demographics.
• Explainability: Doctors must be able to trust and verify why the AI
recommended a specific surgery (Ties into XAI).
Case Study 2: Big Data AI Solution in Smart Manufacturing
• Data Sources: PLCs (Programmable Logic Controllers), vibration
sensors, acoustic sensors, and thermal cameras.
• The Protocol Challenge: Converting diverse industrial protocols
(Modbus, OPC-UA) into Big Data formats (JSON/Avro).
• Ingestion: Using Apache Kafka to handle high-frequency sensor
streams (thousands of events per second).
Deep Dive 1 – Predictive Maintenance (PdM)
• The Logic: Analyzing time-series data to identify "Signatures of
Failure.“
• Algorithm: Using LSTMs (Long Short-Term Memory) or GRUs to find
patterns in vibration and temperature.
• Impact: Shifting from "Fixing when broken" to "Fixing just before
breaking," saving millions in lost production time.
Deep Dive 2 – Computer Vision for Quality 4.0
• The Challenge: Human inspectors can only see 80-90% of defects and
suffer from fatigue.
• The Technology: Deep Learning (CNNs) deployed at the Edge for sub-
millisecond inspection.
• The Workflow: Every single part is photographed; the AI compares it
against a "Perfect Part" model in real-time.
The "Digital Twin" Concept
• A virtual representation of a physical asset updated with real-time Big
Data.
• Simulation: Testing "What If" scenarios in the digital world before
changing the physical machine.
• Integration: Syncing Spark streaming data with 3D CAD models.
Edge-to-Cloud Architecture for Industry
• Edge: Critical for safety (E-stops, high-speed sorting).
• Cloud: Critical for training models across multiple global factories.
• Latency Requirements: Decisions must be made in <10ms for high-
speed robotics.
Economic and Operational Impact
• Energy Optimization: AI identifies power-hungry machines and
optimizes their duty cycles.
• Supply Chain Integration: The factory automatically orders its own
spare parts when the AI predicts a failure.
• The Workforce: Shifting human roles from "Manual Labor" to "AI
Oversight and Maintenance."
